# Title & Abstract

## Title
**Genetically predicted immune-cell and cytokine traits and risk of osteomyelitis, including spine-specific subtypes: a two-sample Mendelian randomization study**

## Running title
Immune traits and osteomyelitis risk: a spine-specific MR study

## Abstract

Spine-specific osteomyelitis — vertebral osteomyelitis (M46.2) and discitis (M46.4) — is hard to study causally, and whether anything in the circulating immune system drives who gets it is unknown. We performed two-sample Mendelian randomization of 11 immune and cytokine traits (EBI GWAS Catalog, GRCh38) against five FinnGen R11 osteomyelitis phenotypes, four spine-specific, using genome-wide instruments (P<5×10⁻⁸, F>10), IVW as primary, and MR-Egger, weighted median/mode, Cochran Q, Egger intercept, leave-one-out, Steiger directionality, power and meta-analysis. Across 45 exposure–outcome pairs nothing survived multiple-testing correction or pleiotropy-robust estimators; in the well-powered outcomes (all-site osteomyelitis n=2,125; discitis n=495) minimum detectable ORs at 80% power were 1.2–3.0, and every estimate was null. Three nominally significant IVW signals in the smallest outcome (n=68) collapsed under robustness checks and pointed in opposite directions across phenotypes, consistent with small-sample noise. Steiger tests supported the exposure→outcome direction throughout; drug-target MR for IL-6 receptor and CD40, and multivariable MR adjusting for BMI, type-2 diabetes and smoking, were null. To our knowledge this is the first MR using spine-specific osteomyelitis GWAS, indicating that circulating immune effectors do not robustly cause osteomyelitis and that clinical triangulation should precede therapeutic claims.
