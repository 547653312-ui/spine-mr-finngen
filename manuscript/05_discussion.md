# Discussion

**What we found.** Across 45 exposure–outcome pairs in FinnGen R11, including four spine-specific phenotypes that have not been used in MR before, we found no robust causal effect of genetically predicted immune-cell counts or circulating cytokine/chemokine levels on osteomyelitis risk. The only nominally significant signals (three of them) all came from the smallest outcome, "other infective spondylopathies" (n=68). They survived IVW, collapsed under weighted median and weighted mode, and pointed in opposite directions across phenotypes: leukocyte count looked protective in SPONDINF (OR 0.04) yet risk-increasing in discitis (OR 1.41). For the well-powered outcomes (all-site osteomyelitis n=2,125; discitis n=495) the minimum detectable ORs at 80% power were 1.2–3.0 and every estimate was null. Cross-phenotype meta-analysis, excluding the unstable n=68 outcome, was uniformly null; the largest meta-estimate (CD40, pooled OR 1.12, P=0.067) remained non-significant, was unsupported by any single outcome and by the sensitivity meta, and is read as chance. Drug-target MR for IL-6 receptor and CD40 was null. Multivariable MR adjusting for BMI, type-2 diabetes and smoking did not pull a signal out either; the single borderline finding (lymphocyte count → discitis, adjusted OR 0.39, P=0.045) does not survive Bonferroni correction for 14 MVMR tests and sits inside a confidence interval that crosses unity-adjacent values.

**What a null MR does and does not mean here.** A null MR is not a verdict that immunity is irrelevant to osteomyelitis. What it says is more limited: the life-course, genetically-predicted level of these circulating immune effectors is not a strong, directional causal determinant of *who* develops osteomyelitis. Three non-mutually-exclusive explanations are worth sitting with:

- **Redundancy.** Host defence against osteomyelitis pathogens (predominantly *S. aureus*, and *M. tuberculosis* in the spinal form) is spread across complement, neutrophil, macrophage and adaptive axes. The genetic proxy of any single circulating trait captures a sliver of this network, and a real but distributed effect is likely to be diluted below detection⁷.
- **Local control matters more than systemic set-points.** Bone and disc-space infection is governed by the local microenvironment — biofilm, necrotic bone, implant surface, the myeloid infiltrate that arrives in response — more than by what someone's leukocyte or cytokine level looked like a decade before bacteraemia²⁷. MR is a lifecourse instrument; osteomyelitis is an acute event.
- **Acute versus trait-level causation.** Infections are precipitated by transient breaches. MR estimates the directional effect of a *trait*, and a short-window mechanism may simply not show up in a trait-level estimate.

A useful sanity check is the observational literature. Once reverse causation is removed — i.e., once CRP and leukocytosis at presentation are no longer treated as a "cause" of susceptibility — the residual observational signal looks weak. Our null is consistent with that⁸,²⁸.

**Translational implications.** The drug-target MR findings are the part clinicians will probably look at first. Genetically proxied IL-6R blockade (the closest available proxy for tocilizumab) and genetically proxied CD40 perturbation both showed no effect on osteomyelitis or discitis susceptibility. We would not, on this evidence, push IL-6R antagonists as *preventive* immunomodulation for bone infection. Their established role in cytokine-release states and a few inflammatory conditions is unaffected. It is worth noting that MR estimates a life-course effect of genetically determined IL-6R signalling, whereas trials of IL-6R antagonists test short-window pharmacological blockade in acutely ill patients; the two need not disagree, and our null should not be read as evidence against the acute use of these drugs in severe infection. The broader read across nine immune traits is that systemic immune modulation does not look like a productive prevention strategy; the field's research dollars are probably better spent on local strategies (source control, biofilm-active antibiotics, implant-surface engineering) and on host factors our instruments do not cover well — complement variants, neutrophil-function polymorphisms.

**Where this sits in the literature.** A 2025 MR study linked osteomyelitis to infective endocarditis genetically¹⁴ but did not interrogate immune drivers and used a mixed-anatomy definition. To our knowledge this is the first MR to (a) treat immune/cytokine traits as the exposure for osteomyelitis and (b) use spine-specific FinnGen phenotypes (VOM M46.2, discitis M46.4, pyogenic disc infection, other infective spondylopathies). The null here is the first piece of evidence that the immune aetiology of vertebral and disc-space infection is not obviously distinct from osteomyelitis at other sites — a question that was simply unaddressable until these phenotypes existed in FinnGen.

**Why we are foregrounding the SPONDINF signals.** The three nominally significant associations are easy to over-read, and we deliberately flag that. They live in the smallest outcome (n=68), survive only IVW, are non-significant under median/mode, and invert direction across phenotypes. That is a textbook small-sample artefact¹²,²⁵. Reporting them transparently — rather than as a protective effect of leukocytosis — is, honestly, one of the more useful things this paper does, given how many recent MR findings come from small outcomes and do not survive the same robustness checks¹⁸.

**Strengths, and what the limits still are.** Strengths: spine-specific exposure framing that has not been done before; the full pre-specified sensitivity stack (IVW + Egger + median + mode, Cochran Q, Egger intercept, leave-one-out, Steiger directionality, power-aware interpretation); FDR/Bonferroni discipline across 45 tests; an open reproducible pipeline that needs no tokenised data; a formal LD-clumping sensitivity analysis using the 1000 Genomes Phase 3 European panel; drug-target MR for two clinically relevant targets; multivariable adjustment for the three metabolic/behavioral confounders we set out to test. Limitations, detailed in the dedicated Limitations section, are real and worth being specific about: the primary analysis used distance pruning in place of formal LD clumping (mitigated by the LD-clumping sensitivity analysis), reverse-direction MR is blocked by current spine-infection GWAS power, the CRP exposure has a multi-ancestry mismatch with the Finnish outcomes, the result generalises to European-ancestry populations only, IL1B and CXCL10 were excluded due to harmonisation failure (so the adaptive T-cell arm of immunity is under-covered), MR carries its standard assumption dependencies, and this is an in-silico summary-statistic study with no individual-level or wet-lab validation. Even with those, the spine-specific design plus the sensitivity stack are what carry the negative conclusion — not any single estimator.
