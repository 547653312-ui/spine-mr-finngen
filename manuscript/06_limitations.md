# Limitations

1. **Instrument independence approximated by distance pruning in the primary analysis.** Because no local LD reference panel was available in the execution environment, the primary analysis enforced instrument independence with a ±500 kb distance filter rather than formal LD clumping. We addressed this directly in a sensitivity analysis: formal LD clumping against the 1000 Genomes Phase 3 European panel (r²<0.001, via LDlink LDmatrix) removed no instrument for five of the six key traits and removed only redundant IL6R variants, leaving every estimate null and directionally unchanged. Residual linkage at loci outside the six key traits cannot be fully excluded, though the LD-clumping concordance, the consistency of results across estimators, and the Cochran Q statistics all mitigate this concern.

2. **Under-powered vertebral and spondylopathy subtypes.** Vertebral osteomyelitis (VOM, n=104) and "other infective spondylopathies" (SPONDINF, n=68) could only detect very large effects (minimum detectable OR 2.5–42 at 80% power). We therefore cannot rule out *modest* causal effects in these spine-specific subtypes; the null there is non-informative rather than conclusive. The better-powered all-site osteomyelitis (n=2,125) and discitis (n=495) analyses are the evidentiary backbone of the negative conclusion.

3. **Reverse-direction MR not feasible.** FinnGen R11 spine-infection phenotypes report <3 genome-wide-significant independent loci per outcome (OM=2, DISCITIS=1, DISCINF=1, VOM=0, SPONDINF=0), precluding a well-powered reverse-direction MR (spine infection → immune traits). The feasibility table (Supplementary Table S6) records the locus counts transparently. A reverse MR should be revisited in FinnGen R12+ or larger spine-infection GWAS as case counts grow.

4. **CRP exposure population mismatch.** The C-reactive protein GWAS (EFO_0004458) is multi-ancestry (Wojcik 2019, PAGE), whereas outcomes are Finnish (European). Allele frequencies and LD differ, so CRP results were *a priori* downgraded to exploratory and interpreted cautiously. All other exposures were European, matching the outcome sample.

5. **European (FinnGen) generalisability.** Findings derive from a Finnish cohort; trans-ancestry generalisation is untested. MR estimates are most robust within the ancestry of the instrument–outcome pair, which here is European.

6. **Partial immune landscape.** IL1B (single instrument) and CXCL10 (three instruments) could not be harmonised to any outcome and were excluded, leaving nine of eleven planned traits. The immune axis is therefore incompletely covered (notably lacking a dedicated adaptive T-cell trait and a complement factor).

7. **Drug-target MR limited by cis-pQTL availability.** Only IL6R (2 cis-SNPs) and CD40 (3 cis-SNPs) yielded analysable cis-pQTL instruments; CRP had no cis-pQTL meeting genome-wide significance. The drug-target MR is therefore restricted to two of three pre-specified targets, and the limited number of instruments widens confidence intervals — though the point estimates are clearly null.

8. **MVMR conditional instrument strength for confounders.** In the multivariable MR, conditional F-statistics for the BMI, T2D and smoking confounders were low-to-moderate (range ~1–20), reflecting limited overlap between the immune-trait IV sets and metabolic/behavioral variants. This means the confounder adjustment is technically valid but does not strongly condition on the confounders; residual confounding by these pathways cannot be fully excluded. The adjusted estimates nonetheless agree with the univariable nulls for 13 of 14 pairs; the single borderline MVMR signal (lymphocyte → discitis, P=0.045) does not survive multiple-testing correction and has a wide confidence interval, consistent with residual noise.

9. **Assumption dependence.** MR relies on the exclusion-restriction, relevance and exchangeability assumptions. Although Egger intercepts showed no directional pleiotropy and Steiger tests supported the assumed direction, balanced (non-directional) pleiotropy or horizontal pleiotropy undetectable by these tests remains possible.

10. **No individual-level or experimental validation.** This is an in-silico study; conclusions rest on summary-statistic MR. As recommended in the Discussion, triangulation with clinical cohorts and (where ethical) experimental models is needed before any translational claim. The study design itself includes no wet-lab component, consistent with the purely computational scope.

11. **Multiple outcomes, correlated.** The five osteomyelitis phenotypes are related (all capture bone/disc infection); their separate analysis slightly inflates family-wise testing. We addressed this via FDR/Bonferroni correction across 45 tests and a cross-phenotype meta-analysis, but the phenotypes are not independent.

12. **MVMR retrieval toolchain.** The confounder summary statistics were retrieved via a custom BGZF random-access reader against the full FinnGen R11 files. To ensure stable retrieval for scattered genome-wide immune-cell-count instrument positions, the reader created a fresh FinnGenSumstats object per query (the .tbi tabix index is cached on disk, so re-construction is cheap); the 128-KB read window at the linear index offset was sufficient for all three confounders across all instrument SNPs after this fix. The reader stringifies chromosome identifiers as `str(int(float(chrom)))` to avoid a `1.0`-vs-`1` mismatch that would otherwise return empty hits in the tabix lookup; this is a documented robustness check.
