## Title
**Genetically predicted immune-cell and cytokine traits and risk of osteomyelitis, including spine-specific subtypes: a two-sample Mendelian randomization study**

## Abstract

Spine-specific osteomyelitis — vertebral osteomyelitis (M46.2) and discitis (M46.4) — is a hard disease to study causally, and we do not know whether anything in the circulating immune system actually drives who gets it. We used two-sample Mendelian randomization on 11 immune and cytokine traits (EBI GWAS Catalog, GRCh38) against five FinnGen R11 osteomyelitis phenotypes, four spine-specific. We used genome-wide IVs (P<5×10⁻⁸, F>10) with IVW as primary, plus MR-Egger, weighted median/mode, Cochran Q, Egger intercept, leave-one-out, Steiger directionality, power and meta-analysis. Across 45 exposure–outcome pairs nothing survived multiple-testing correction or the pleiotropy-robust estimators. In the well-powered outcomes (all-site osteomyelitis n=2,125; discitis n=495) minimum detectable ORs at 80% power were 1.2–3.0 and every estimate was null. Three nominally significant IVW signals in the smallest outcome (n=68) collapsed under robustness and pointed in opposite directions across phenotypes, consistent with small-sample noise. Steiger tests supported the exposure→outcome direction throughout. Drug-target MR for IL-6 receptor and CD40, and multivariable MR adjusting for BMI, type-2 diabetes and smoking, were both null. To our knowledge this is the first MR using spine-specific osteomyelitis GWAS, suggesting that circulating immune effectors do not robustly cause osteomyelitis and that clinical triangulation should precede therapeutic claims.
