# Cover Letter — Scientific Reports

**[Date]**

**Dear Editors,**

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, **"Genetically predicted immune-cell and cytokine traits and risk of osteomyelitis, including spine-specific subtypes: a two-sample Mendelian randomization study,"** for consideration as an original research article in *Scientific Reports*.

**What this study adds.** This is, to our knowledge, the first Mendelian randomization (MR) study to interrogate the immune determinants of osteomyelitis using the spine-specific phenotypes that became available in FinnGen R11 — vertebral osteomyelitis (M46.2), discitis (M46.4), pyogenic intervertebral-disc infection, and other infective spondylopathies — in addition to all-site osteomyelitis. Prior MR work on osteomyelitis has relied on mixed-anatomy definitions that cannot address spine-specific biology.

**What we found, and why it is informative.** Across 45 exposure–outcome pairs (9 immune/cytokine traits × 5 outcomes), a rigorously executed null result emerged: no association survived multiple-testing correction or the pleiotropy-robust estimators. Crucially, this is not a power problem — for the well-powered outcomes (all-site osteomyelitis n=2,125; discitis n=495) the minimum detectable odds ratios at 80% power were 1.2–3.0, and every estimate was null. Three nominally significant signals confined to the smallest outcome (n=68) collapsed under weighted-median/weighted-mode estimators and inverted direction across phenotypes — a textbook small-sample artefact that we report transparently rather than hide. Sensitivity analyses were comprehensive: MR-Egger, weighted median/mode, Cochran Q, Egger intercept, leave-one-out, Steiger directionality, post-hoc power, cross-phenotype meta-analysis, drug-target (cis-pQTL) MR for IL-6 receptor and CD40, and multivariable MR adjusting for BMI, type-2 diabetes and smoking. A formal LD-clumping sensitivity analysis using the 1000 Genomes Phase 3 European panel confirmed the instrument independence assumed by the primary distance-pruned analysis.

**Clinical relevance.** The drug-target MR is the translational core: genetically proxied IL-6R blockade and CD40 perturbation showed no causal effect on osteomyelitis or discitis susceptibility, arguing against the use of IL-6R antagonists or emerging anti-CD40 biologics as preventive immunomodulation for bone infection. A null result with this sensitivity stack is, we believe, exactly the kind of evidence that protects the field from pursuing dead-end therapeutic avenues.

**Reproducibility.** The full analysis pipeline (pure Python, no authentication tokens, no restricted data) is publicly available at https://github.com/547653312-ui/spine-mr-finngen with a persistent identifier at Zenodo (DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21863390), including every per-SNP harmonisation table, leave-one-out and single-SNP estimates, the 188 per-pair supplementary panels, and instrument manifests. The study is reported in accordance with STROBE-MR.

This manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors have approved the submission and declare no competing interests.

We believe this work will be of broad interest to *Scientific Reports*' readership in infectious diseases, orthopaedics, immunology and genetic epidemiology, and we thank you for considering it.
